# Well-being research corpus

Synthesized from two rounds of deep research on Santos' Science of Well-Being framework. Every claim traced to primary investigators. Sample sizes included for all studies where available.

---

## A. Cognitive biases — why the brain gets happiness wrong

Santos' course starts by dismantling these biases. They explain why the practices in Section B work, and why "just knowing" doesn't help.

### A1. Hedonic adaptation

You adapt to good things and stop noticing them. The brain treats improvements as the new normal.

**Primary research:**
- **Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman (1978)** — "Lottery Winners and Accident Victims: Is Happiness Relative?", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. N=73 (22 lottery winners, 29 accident victims, 22 controls). Lottery winners rated happiness 4.00/5.00 vs controls 3.82/5.00 — not significantly different. Famous but small study.
- **Diener, Lucas & Scollon (2006)** — "Beyond the Hedonic Treadmill," *American Psychologist*. Review of longitudinal panels, N>10,000. Confirmed adaptation exists but is not always complete — severe negative shocks can permanently lower the baseline.
- **Kahneman (1999)** — Peak-End Rule. Evaluation of past experiences is dominated by peak intensity and final moments, not the average.

**Santos' teaching:** Weeks 2 and 4 of Coursera course. "We get used to stuff but don't know it."

**Actionable implication:** Spend on experiences over possessions (experiences fade from memory, keeping them special; possessions stay present, accelerating adaptation). Vary positive activities to prevent staleness.

### A2. Miswanting and the focusing illusion

People consistently overestimate how much future events will affect their mood, and for how long.

**Primary research:**
- **Wilson & Gilbert (2005)** — "Affective Forecasting," *Current Directions in Psychological Science*. Thousands of participants across multiple studies. People overestimate impact of both positive and negative events.
- **Schkade & Kahneman (1998)** — "Does Living in California Make People Happy?", *Psychological Science*. N=1,903 students. No actual satisfaction difference between Midwest and California residents. "Nothing is as important as you think it is while you're thinking about it."

**Santos' teaching:** Weeks 1 and 3. The GI Joe Fallacy — knowing about these biases doesn't fix them.

### A3. Reference points and social comparison

The brain evaluates outcomes relative to other people, not in absolute terms.

**Primary research:**
- **Solnick & Hemenway (1998)** — "Is More Always Better?", *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*. N=257. ~50% preferred earning $50k (others earn $25k) over $100k (others earn $250k).
- **Easterlin (1974)** — The Easterlin Paradox. As national GDP grows, national happiness doesn't — reference standards rise in tandem.
- **Twenge (2017)** — *iGen* + World Happiness Report. N>500,000 US/UK adolescents. Heavy social media (5+ hrs/day) doubles depression risk. Well-being peaks at 0.5-2 hours leisure screen time.

### A4. The happiness pie (revised)

**Lyubomirsky, Sheldon & Schkade (2005)** — "Pursuing Happiness: The Architecture of Sustainable Change," *Review of General Psychology*, 9(2), 111-131. Original model: 50% genetics, 10% circumstances, 40% intentional activity. Based on twin registries and demographic datasets.

**Critique:** Brown & Rohrer (2020), *Journal of Happiness Studies*. The 50/10/40 decomposition assumes the three factors don't interact — statistically flawed.

**Revision:** Sheldon & Lyubomirsky (2021), *Journal of Positive Psychology*. Retired the pie chart but maintained the core premise: intentional activities can sustainably alter well-being if practiced with variety and continued effort. Typical effect sizes smaller than initially assumed (Hedges' g = 0.17-0.19).

**For the skill:** The exact percentages don't matter. The message: a meaningful fraction of your well-being responds to what you actually do — but only if you practice, not just know.

### A5. Negativity bias and loss aversion

Bad is stronger than good. Preventing negatives matters more than adding positives.

**Primary research:**
- **Baumeister et al. (2001)** — "Bad Is Stronger Than Good," *Review of General Psychology*. Synthesis of 200+ studies. Negative events produce larger, faster, more durable effects than equivalent positive ones across all domains studied.
- **Kahneman & Tversky (1979)** — Prospect Theory, *Econometrica*. Loss aversion coefficient 1.5-2.5x: losing $100 hurts roughly twice as much as gaining $100 feels good.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 4. "A single bad day can wipe out a week of counting blessings."

**For the skill:** This is the foundation for Tier 1 priority ordering. Fix sleep debt, social isolation, and digital overconsumption BEFORE adding gratitude journaling. One bad night of sleep erases a week of positive practices.

### A6. The GI Joe Fallacy

Knowing about biases does not fix them. Just like knowing the Muller-Lyer lines are equal doesn't make them look equal.

**Source:** Santos & Gendler (2014), Edge.org; WIPHI Critical Thinking Series (2015).

**For the skill:** This is why the program prescribes practices with structure (WOOP, environmental design, schedules) rather than just explaining the research. The entire skill exists because of this fallacy.

## B. Practices — with dosing, sample sizes, and failure modes

### Tier 1: Fix negatives first

#### B1. Sleep as mood foundation

**Primary research:**
- **Kahneman et al. (2004)** — Day Reconstruction Method, *Science*. N=909 working women. Sleep quality was the single strongest predictor of next-day mood — stronger than income, marital status, or job satisfaction.
- **Twenge (2017)** — N>500,000. Phone-in-bedroom is one of the biggest sleep killers for well-being.

**Dose:** 7-8 hours per night. Phone out of bedroom. No screens 1 hour before bed.

**Failure mode:** "Just checking my phone for a minute" in bed — triggers dopamine, disrupts circadian rhythm.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 4 rewirement.

#### B2. Social connection

**Primary research:**
- **Diener & Seligman (2002)** — "Very Happy People," *Psychological Science*. N=222. The one trait all very happy people shared: strong social relationships.
- **Holt-Lunstad et al. (2010)** — Meta-analysis, *PLOS Medicine*. N=308,849 across 148 studies. Low social connection = mortality risk equivalent to smoking 15 cigarettes/day.
- **Epley & Schroeder (2014)** — "Mistakenly Seeking Solitude," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*. ~200 per condition. Chicago commuters assigned to talk to strangers reported significantly more positive experiences than solitude group. Effect size d=0.79 to 1.57.

**Dose:** 1 meaningful face-to-face conversation per week (60 min). 1 brief daily interaction with a stranger or acquaintance.

**Failure mode:** Phone checking during conversations ("phubbing") degrades connection quality.

**Santos' teaching:** Weeks 3 and 5.

#### B3. Digital hygiene

**Primary research:**
- **Twenge (2017)** — N>500,000 US/UK adolescents. Well-being peaks at 0.5-2 hours of leisure screen time per day, drops steadily after. Heavy users (5+ hrs) twice as likely to show depressive symptoms.
- Key distinction: passive algorithmic scrolling is the problem. Video calls with close people count as social connection, not screen time.

**Dose:** Cap recreational screen time at 2 hours/day. Phone out of bedroom at night.

**Failure mode:** Late-night unstructured browsing bypasses cognitive control.

**Santos' teaching:** Covered in teens course and Twenge podcast interview.

### Tier 2: Active practices

#### B4. Gratitude journal

**Primary research:**
- **Emmons & McCullough (2003)** — "Counting Blessings Versus Burdens," *JPSP*. N=192. 10-week RCT. Gratitude group reported higher positive affect, fewer complaints.
- **Lyubomirsky, Sheldon & Schkade (2005)** — *Review of General Psychology*. N=82 women. Once/week journaling increased happiness significantly (F(1,65)=4.41, p=0.04). Three times/week showed NO improvement — became rote.
- **Meta-analysis** across 145 papers, 24,804 participants: Hedges' g=0.19 (reliable but small).

**Dose:** 5 specific things, once per week (e.g., Sunday evening). 10 minutes. Specificity matters: "my friend drove 40 minutes to help me move" beats "family."

**Failure mode:** Daily practice makes it rote and kills the effect. Also: generic entries ("health, family, job") produce nothing.

**Cross-cultural flag:** Worked well in US samples but triggered indebtedness/guilt in South Korean samples (Layous et al. 2013, N=520). If it feels like obligation rather than warmth after 2-3 weeks, swap to acts of kindness.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 2 rewirement.

#### B5. Gratitude visit

**Primary research:**
- **Seligman et al. (2005)** — "Positive Psychology Progress," *American Psychologist*. N=411. Write a 300-word letter to someone never properly thanked, read aloud in person or on video. Produced the largest immediate happiness boost of all interventions tested. Effect still measurable at 1 month.

**Dose:** Once per quarter (~90 days).

**Cross-cultural flag:** Same as gratitude journal — may trigger indebtedness in some people. Normalize dropping it.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 5 rewirement.

#### B6. Acts of kindness

**Primary research:**
- **Lyubomirsky, Tkach & Sheldon (2004/2005)** — *Review of General Psychology*. N=117. 5 acts of kindness clustered on a single day/week produced a significant happiness boost (F(1,101)=4.31, p=0.04). Same 5 acts spread across the week: no effect.
- **Dunn, Aknin & Norton (2008)** — "Spending Money on Others Promotes Happiness," *Science*. Initial N=46, replicated at N=712 and N=5,199. Prosocial spending reliably boosts happiness more than spending on self.

**Dose:** 5 acts, all on one day per week.

**Why clustering works (scaffolding framing):** Scattered acts blend into routine and go unnoticed. Clustering makes you notice yourself being kind — the self-perception shift is the mechanism. Once you start seeing yourself as a kind person, the structure becomes invisible.

**Failure mode:** Spreading acts across the week (blends into routine). Also: forcing acts that feel performative rather than genuine.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 3 rewirement.

#### B7. Meditation

**Primary research:**
- **Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010)** — "A Wandering Mind Is an Unhappy Mind," *Science*. N=2,250 adults via smartphone sampling. Minds wander 46.9% of the time. Mind-wandering predicts unhappiness regardless of activity.
- **Fredrickson et al. (2008)** — "Open Hearts Build Lives," *JPSP*. N=139 working adults. Loving-kindness meditation (LKM) increased daily positive emotions (d=0.40-0.50), built lasting personal resources.
- **Davidson & Lutz (2008)** — Neuroimaging studies. Regular meditation produces cortical thickening, reduced amygdala reactivity.
- **Kabat-Zinn (1990)** — MBSR program. Hundreds of clinical patients. Medium-to-large reductions in stress and anxiety.

**Dose:** 10 min/day, breath-focused. After 4 weeks, add 1 loving-kindness session per week (silently wish well-being for self, loved ones, strangers, difficult people).

**Failure mode:** Treating mind-wandering during meditation as failure — triggers self-criticism, leads to quitting. Mind-wandering IS the exercise; noticing it and returning is the rep.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 5 rewirement.

#### B8. Savoring

**Primary research:**
- **Bryant & Veroff (2007)** — *Savoring: A New Model of Positive Experience*. Three types: anticipatory, in-the-moment, reminiscent. Five techniques: sharing, mental photography, self-congratulation, sensory sharpening, avoiding dampening.
- **Mauss et al. (2011)** — *Emotion*. N=255 (135 community adults + 120 undergraduates). Monitoring whether savoring is "making you happy" kills the positive effect. People who highly valued happiness reported MORE depressive symptoms.

**Dose:** One 5-minute savoring moment per day. Vary what you savor.

**Failure mode:** Self-monitoring ("am I happy yet?") — this is the Mauss paradox. Do not check whether it's working.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 2 rewirement.

#### B9. Awe walk

**Primary research:**
- **Sturm et al. (2020)** — "Big Smile, Small Self," *Emotion*. N=60 healthy older adults (ages 60-90). 8-week RCT. Weekly awe walks increased prosocial emotions, broadened smile intensity, reduced daily distress. Small sample but well-measured (behavioral coding + daily diaries).
- **Keltner (2023)** — *Awe: The New Science of Everyday Wonder*. Awe identified across 26 countries. Triggers: nature, moral beauty, music, collective effervescence.

**Dose:** 15 min/week. Novel location when possible. Phone OFF — only works with outward attention.

**Failure mode:** Treating it as a workout (focusing on speed/steps). Also: phone on.

**Santos' teaching:** Covered in The Happiness Lab podcast.

### Tier 3: Deeper design

#### B10. Signature strengths

**Primary research:**
- **Peterson & Seligman (2004)** — VIA Classification. 24 character strengths across 6 virtues. Global psychometric validation.
- **Seligman et al. (2005)** — *American Psychologist*. N=411. Using a signature strength in a new way daily for one week produced a happiness boost lasting 6 months — one of the longest-lasting effects in the literature.

**Dose:** Take VIA survey (free, 240 items). Use one top strength in a new way each day for 1 week. Cycle through top 5.

**Failure mode:** Repeating the same application daily (hedonic adaptation).

**Santos' teaching:** Weeks 1-2 rewirement.

#### B11. Flow design

**Primary research:**
- **Csikszentmihalyi (1990)** — *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience*. Full absorption in challenging-but-matched activity. Self-consciousness and time sense disappear. Diverse global populations (artists, athletes, surgeons, assembly workers).
- **Seligman (2002)** — *Authentic Happiness*. The "engaged life" (flow) contributes more to lasting well-being than the "pleasant life" (hedonic pleasures).

**Dose:** One 90-minute distraction-free flow block per week minimum.

**Protocol:** Audit activities on challenge vs. skill. High challenge + low skill = anxiety (break task down). Low challenge + high skill = boredom (add constraints). Balance = flow.

**Failure mode:** Multitasking. Phone notifications break immersion.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 5.

#### B12. Intrinsic goal re-alignment

**Primary research:**
- **Kasser (2002)** — *The High Price of Materialism*. People prioritizing extrinsic goals (money, fame, image) report higher anxiety, lower self-esteem, lower life satisfaction.
- **Berridge & Robinson (1998)** — *Brain Research Reviews*. "Wanting" (dopamine) and "liking" (opioid) are separate brain systems. You can intensely want something you won't enjoy once you get it.
- **Sheldon & Houser-Marko (2001)** — Self-concordance model. Goals aligned with authentic values produce sustained effort and lasting well-being.

**Protocol:** Audit top 3 goals. Rate each 1 (extrinsic) to 7 (intrinsic). Reframe extrinsic goals toward growth/connection/contribution components.

**Santos' teaching:** Weeks 2 and 6.

#### B13. Time affluence

**Primary research:**
- **Whillans et al. (2017)** — *PNAS*. N=6,271 across US, Canada, Denmark, Netherlands. Spending money to buy time (outsourcing disliked chores) increases life satisfaction (d=0.24). Effect holds regardless of income.
- **Mogilner, Chance & Norton (2012)** — *Psychological Science*. N=218. Giving time away (volunteering) makes you feel you have MORE time — boosts sense of personal effectiveness.

**Dose:** Outsource 1 disliked task per month. Volunteer 1 hour per week.

**Failure mode:** Guilt over outsourcing prevents enjoying reclaimed time. Also: reinvesting saved time into passive scrolling instead of connection/hobbies/rest.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 5.

#### B14. Meaning and purpose

**Primary research:**
- **Seligman (2011)** — *Flourish*. PERMA model: Meaning is a distinct pillar, separate from pleasure, engagement, relationships, or accomplishment.
- **Steger et al. (2006)** — Meaning in Life Questionnaire, *Journal of Counseling Psychology*. N=1,183. "Presence of meaning" strongly correlated with life satisfaction (r=0.46) and negatively with depression (r=-0.48). "Search for meaning" (without presence) can temporarily increase anxiety.
- **Frankl (1959)** — *Man's Search for Meaning*. Historical influence on positive psychology. "He who has a why to live can bear almost any how."

**Protocol:** Identify top 3 values. Audit whether weekly actions align. Commit 1 hour/week to a values-aligned contribution (mentoring, volunteering, community project).

**Failure mode:** Pursuing meaning purely for self-validation shifts motivation and degrades the benefit.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 5 (eudaimonic framing).

#### B15. Self-compassion

**Primary research:**
- **Neff (2003)** — "Self-Compassion: An Alternative Conceptualization," *Self and Identity*. Three components: self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness. Strongly negatively correlated with depression (r=-0.52) and anxiety (r=-0.51).
- **Neff & Germer (2013)** — Mindful Self-Compassion (MSC) 8-week program. RCT. Significant increases in self-compassion, mindfulness, life satisfaction. Improvements durable at 1-year follow-up.
- **COI flag:** Neff and Germer co-founded the Center for Mindful Self-Compassion (commercial entity). Flag in Sources section.

**Dose:** 5-minute self-compassion break when triggered by self-criticism. Three steps: (1) Notice and name the pain ("this is a moment of suffering"), (2) Normalize ("everyone fails — this is part of being human"), (3) Befriend ("what would I say to a friend in this situation?").

**Failure mode:** Using self-compassion to avoid accountability (rare but possible). Self-compassion is about safe acknowledgment of failure, not ignoring it.

**Santos' teaching:** Covered in The Happiness Lab Season 2.

## C. Behavior change — making it stick

### C1. WOOP (Wish-Outcome-Obstacle-Plan)

**Primary research:**
- **Oettingen (2014)** — *Rethinking Positive Thinking*. The WOOP protocol: Wish → Outcome (visualize) → Obstacle (identify internal barrier) → Plan (if-then).
- **Gollwitzer (1999)** — "Implementation Intentions," *American Psychologist*. Meta-analysis of 94 studies (thousands of participants). If-then planning roughly doubles goal follow-through (d≈0.65).
- **Oettingen (2014), Ch.5, pp.112-115** — WOOP fails when obstacles are framed as external ("my boss scheduled a meeting") rather than internal ("I'll feel too tired"). Internal obstacles are required for the if-then link to form.

**Dose:** 5 minutes each morning. One wish, one if-then plan.

**Santos' teaching:** Week 6.

### C2. Person-Activity Fit

**Primary research:**
- **Lyubomirsky & Sheldon** — Person-Activity Fit Diagnostic. Rate activities on Natural, Enjoyable, Valuable (positive) minus Guilty, Situational pressure (negative). Fit Score = (N+E+V)/3 - (G+S)/2. Pick the 4 highest-scoring practices.
- **Lyubomirsky & Layous (2013)** — Positive Activity Model, *Human Development*. Success depends on both activity features (dosing, variety) and person features (personality, motivation).

**For the skill:** The intake's "tried and dropped" question and starting preference question serve as a lightweight fit assessment. Don't prescribe practices the user already bounced off of (unless the issue was fixable dosing — e.g., they tried daily gratitude and burned out, but weekly might work).

### C3. Hedonic Adaptation Prevention (HAP)

**Primary research:**
- **Sheldon, Boehm & Lyubomirsky (2013)** — "Variety is the Spice of Happiness," *Oxford Handbook of Happiness*. N=120+ undergraduates over 10 weeks. Participants who varied their kind acts maintained happiness boost. Those who repeated the same acts saw it decay completely.

**For the skill:** Every practice in the program output should include a variety suggestion. "Alternate written gratitude, verbal gratitude, and gratitude letters. Change your savoring targets. Use different strengths."

### C4. Environmental design

**Source:** Santos, Week 6. Situation selection beats willpower. Phone in another room > willpower not to check it. Running shoes by door > motivation to exercise.

**For the skill:** Include one personalized environmental design tip based on the user's identified negative leaks.

## D. Contextual factors

### D1. Money and happiness

**Primary research:**
- **Kahneman & Deaton (2010)** — *PNAS*. N>450,000 (Gallup survey). Emotional well-being plateaus at ~$75k/year. Life evaluation keeps rising.
- **Killingsworth (2021)** — *PNAS*. N=33,391 (1.7M smartphone reports). Experienced well-being rises linearly with log(income), no plateau.
- **Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers (2023)** — Adversarial collaboration, *PNAS*. Resolution: for 80% of people, happiness keeps rising with income. For the unhappiest 20%, it flattens at ~$100k. Overall correlation: r=0.09.

**For the skill:** See money framing in SKILL.md. Lead with r=0.09. Functional threshold (financial stress eliminated). Pivot to smart spending (time, experiences, health infrastructure, prosocial).

### D2. Culture

**Primary research:**
- **Layous et al. (2013)** — *Journal of Happiness Studies*. N=520 (250 US, 270 South Korean). Gratitude letters worked for Americans, backfired for South Koreans (triggered indebtedness). Acts of kindness worked equally in both.
- **Boehm & Lyubomirsky (2011)** — *Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology*. N=279 (142 Anglo American, 137 Asian American). Anglo-Americans showed larger gains from positive interventions.

**For the skill:** No nationality gating. Flag inconsistent practices (gratitude journaling/letters). Normalize stopping. Acts of kindness are the universal fallback.

### D3. Age and life stage

**Primary research:**
- **Blanchflower & Oswald (2008)** — *Social Science & Medicine*. N>500,000 across 80 countries. U-curve: happiness dips in midlife (40-50), rises in later years.
- **Carstensen (1999)** — Socioemotional Selectivity Theory. As time horizon narrows, people prioritize emotional meaning over novelty and status.

**Age-sensitive recommendations:**
- Young adults: digital hygiene, sleep habits, intrinsic goal setting
- Midlife: time affluence, strengths, meaning/purpose
- Older adults: deep connection, reminiscent savoring, volunteering

### D4. The happiness paradox

**Primary research:**
- **Mauss et al. (2011)** — *Emotion*. N=255. Highly valuing happiness is associated with more depressive symptoms. Self-monitoring kills the effect.
- **Ford & Mauss (2018)** — *Emotion*. Toxic positivity: cultural pressure to feel positive leads to worse outcomes.

**For the skill:** This is why the tone framing matters so much. Never say "this will make you happy." Frame benefits as side effects described in human terms. Include the anti-monitoring warning.
