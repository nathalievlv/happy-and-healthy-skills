## Core philosophy

- **Sedentary aging vs. true biological aging.** Most "normal aging" is sedentary aging — preventable cascade of functional deterioration from chronic inactivity and metabolic neglect. Performance declines <2%/yr from age 50–75 in active populations; sharp deceleration to >8%/yr starts after 75.
- **Critical decade.** Women 35–45, men 40–50 are the window for first baseline labs, first physical, first screening. Wright pushes earlier than most national guidelines authorize free coverage — out-of-pocket may be needed.
- **Optimal vs. normal.** Standard lab reference ranges are derived from sedentary, aging, sub-optimally-fed populations. Target *optimal* values, not just "in range."
- **Sleep is the #1 pillar.** Wright explicitly evolved her hierarchy: from putting mobility first (Fitness After 40 era) to putting sleep first (Unbreakable era). "You can't do any of these other things without a well-recovered brain."

## Five Pillars (Wright's framework)

1. **Sleep** — primary foundation. Governs HGH release, memory consolidation, glymphatic clearance.
2. **Exercise** — structural preservation. Mechanical loading + metabolic demand.
3. **Nutrition** — biochemical signaling. Anti-inflammatory, microbiome-supportive, protein-anchored.
4. **Stress management** — endocrine regulation. Cortisol → telomere shortening, visceral adiposity.
5. **Toxins & supplements** — cellular clearance. EDCs out; senolytics in to clear "zombie cells."

## Six Time Bombs of Aging (Wright's biological framework)

1. **Mitochondrial dysfunction** → countered by Zone 2 training.
2. **Telomere shortening** → countered by reducing inflammation, sleep, purpose-driven mobility.
3. **Stem cell decline** → countered by heavy protein pacing, collagen, heavy resistance training.
4. **Chronic inflammation ("inflammaging")** → countered by anti-inflammatory diet, EDC avoidance.
5. **Senescent "zombie" cells** → countered by senolytics (fisetin, quercetin).
6. **Tissue elasticity loss** → countered by HRT, dynamic flexibility, electrolyte hydration.

## F.A.C.E. (Wright's movement framework)

- **F**lexibility and joint range of motion — daily dynamic warm-ups; preserve BLT movements (Bending, Lifting, Twisting); fight kyphosis with erector spinae and rectus abdominis work (planks, bird-dogs).
- **A**erobic fitness and VO2 max — 4 days/wk Zone 2 base (≈ 60–65% effort, ~130 bpm, ~3 hr/wk) + 2 days/wk sprint intervals.
- **C**arry a load — heavy resistance training. "Lift heavy iron." Wright openly disdains the "pink weight" phenomenon marketed to women.
- **E**quilibrium and foot speed — balance and rapid foot-reaction work to prevent falls.

### Specific movement protocols

| Block | Movement | Sets × reps | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Big lifts: squat, deadlift, bench press, overhead press | 4 × 4 | Heavy; metal weights, not bands |
| Accessory | 3-way delts, bicep curls, tricep extensions, lat pulldowns/rows | 4 × 8 (or 8, then 6) | Heavy enough that last 2 reps are difficult |
| Core & Power | Russian twists, hamstring extensions, planks, knee-ups, **box jumps 20–24"** | high intensity, explosive | — |

Cadence: 4 days/week minimum 3 days/week. Beginner ramp **paywalled** in the Unbreakable Lifestyle app — not publicly specified.

**Cardio specifics:**

- Zone 2 base: ~3 hours/week at 65% MHR (~125–130 bpm for Wright herself, lactate-threshold-verified). Example: treadmill speed 4, incline 4–5, 45 minutes, brisk walk.
- Sprint intervals: 2 days/week, 30 seconds at 100% effort (her HR hits ~186), recover 2–3 minutes, 4 rounds. "Burns 40% more fat than HIIT done at 80%."
- **Rucking** with a 20 lb weighted vest: adds ~10 bpm at Z2 pace, osteogenic loading.
- Wright's competency baseline for women: "Every woman should be able to do 11 push-ups" — full version, not knee-modified.

### Bone-impact loading

For osteoblast stimulation: jumping, running, stairs, mini-trampoline (rebounder). For poor bone density: trampoline-style impact.

### Weighted vest

Wright actively champions integrating a weighted vest into **daily activities of living** (chores, dog walking) — not just structured workouts. Starting weight 8–10 lbs. The mechanical stress from contraction-against-load is the signal; while sitting or doing chores, a vest can triple daily caloric expenditure and reduce visceral fat. Note from adjacent literature: vests may be **contraindicated** in women with existing spine fractures or severe hyperkyphosis.

## Mindset Mobilization (Wright's psychological framework)

Four sequential steps:

1. **Vision Crafting** — construct a vivid, tangible vision of your future self at an advanced age (Wright uses 97). Define specific capabilities: walking unassisted, driving, traveling, minimizing time in medical facilities. The average older adult surrenders ~3 weeks/year to medical appointments.
2. **Values identification** — establish the psychological "why" that sustains discipline when motivation wanes.
3. **Goals anchored to values** — surface-level aesthetic goals fail; anchoring physical objectives ("the ability to carry a load") to a core value ("physical capability to carry a grandchild") dramatically increases compliance.
4. **Ruthless Prioritization** — transition from efficiency (checking off tasks) to effectiveness (executing only tasks that move you toward the vision).

### Not-To-Do List

A cornerstone behavior. Eliminate "time vampires" — mindless scrolling, purposeless meetings, guilt-driven obligations. "No" is a complete sentence. Every reluctant yes to external demands is an unintentional no to personal health, recovery, longevity.

### Morning routine

Wright's personal protocol: 5:00–5:30 AM journaling and visualization. Cognitive peak (5 AM – 2 PM) reserved for "deep work"; physical exercise pushed to later in the day to avoid burning peak cerebral ATP on physical exertion. Explicitly contrarian to the mainstream "morning workout" narrative.

This phrase is **NOT** verified as Wright's term (Round 2: third-party paraphrase). Use "Mindset Mobilization" when citing.

## Nutrition

### 30-30-3 Rule (Wright-coined, verified Round 2)

- **30 g of protein** in the first meal of the day.
- **30 g of dietary fiber** distributed throughout the day.
- **3 servings of fermented/probiotic foods** every single day (e.g., sauerkraut, high-quality yogurt).

Effect: lowers systemic inflammation by nearly 20%. The 3 fermented servings drive a "microbiome shift" — the gut directly mediates estrogen and progesterone digestion during the menopausal transition.

### Quantitative targets

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Total protein | **130 g/day** (Wright's personal target) |
| Protein per meal | **20–30 g** minimum per sitting (to trigger muscle protein synthesis) |
| Fiber | 25–30 g daily |
| Complex carbohydrates | ~50 g allocated to the morning (CNS / cognitive fuel) |
| Morning meal type | Protein-only or "hobbit-style double breakfast" — avoid sugary breakfast that spikes glucose and triggers brain fog |
| Hydration (pre-workout) | 20 oz water exactly 1 hour before a run |
| Caffeine cap | **≤400 mg/day, ≤200 mg/sitting**. Strict temporal cutoff (exact hour not codified). |
| Alcohol / lifestyle | "**85/15 rule**" (Wright's correction of the standard 80/20). Allows 15% deviation. Note: this is **not strongly verified** (Round 2: Substack post inaccessible; principle holds). Wright explicitly: "Alcohol is your sleep's biggest enemy." |

### Anti-inflammatory food list (Wright)

**Vegetables:** broccoli, cauliflower, spinach, mushrooms, Brussels sprouts, kelp, leeks, sweet potatoes, turnip greens.

**Fruits:** apples (with skin), papayas, pineapples, grapefruit, guava, kiwis, melons, limes, oranges.

**Herbs & spices:** turmeric, garlic, ginger, basil, black pepper, chives, cilantro, cinnamon, parsley.

**Nuts & seeds:** almonds, flaxseed, hazelnuts, pecans, sesame seeds, sunflower seeds, walnuts.

**Elimination targets:** fried foods, commercial salad dressings (100 cal of fat in 1 tbsp dressing with zero nutritional value), liquid fruit juices (massive glycemic spikes from concentrated fructose without fibrous buffering).

### Protein math (flag the unit inconsistency)

- Wright quotes both **"1.4–2.2 g/kg body weight"** (research-doc framing) AND **"1 g per ideal pound body weight"** (transcript). These are different. Default to the kg-based formulation; mention the discrepancy if explaining.
- **Leucine emphasis:** most powerful BCAA for mTOR; whey ~10% leucine, plant proteins lower.

### Fasting — explicit anti-fasting stance for midlife women (Round 2 verified)

Wright is profoundly skeptical of fasting for midlife women. Active female physiology requires robust glycogen storage; progesterone mediates this. Restrictive caloric diets disrupt hormone production and menstrual cyclicity, causing fasting to "backfire." Wright places herself in direct opposition to male-centric longevity influencers who champion aggressive fasting. She prefers autophagy via **exercise** rather than caloric deprivation. For midlife women: **avoid extended fasting**. (Sims-aligned.)

### Mindful behavioral

Mindful chewing for nutrient absorption; hydration as a non-negotiable foundation for all metabolic processes.

## Hormonal — Women (HRT framework, verified Round 2)

### Wright's HRT recommendations (own platform, verified)

- **Body-identical (bioidentical)** estradiol and **oral micronized progesterone**, not synthetic progestins.
- **Transdermal estradiol** over oral synthetic estrogens — avoids hepatic first-pass metabolism, reduces thrombotic risk while crossing the blood-brain barrier.
- **100 mg of micronized progesterone** for women on continuous estrogen with intact uterus (You Are Not Broken Podcast).
- **Vaginal estrogen** explicitly recommended — safe non-negotiable for localized genitourinary symptoms, even for women not candidates for systemic HRT.
- **Vaginal DHEA (prasterone, Intrarosa)** — "miraculous, albeit expensive"; converted by vaginal tissue into localized estrogen + testosterone. Revitalizes receptors in vulva and lower reproductive tract.
- **Testosterone for women** — Wright calls testosterone "the most critically underprescribed hormone in female longevity and wellness."

### Critical window

**Initiate within 10 years of final menstrual cycle.** Waiting until the textbook age of menopause (51) to start therapy "squanders the critical interventional window, potentially costing women decades of protection." Benefits if initiated in window:

- Cardiovascular disease risk: **-40 to -50%** (Wright cites "Yale study of 120 million women").
- All-cause mortality: **-20 to -40%**.
- Lifespan: +3–4 years.

### Unique role of estrogen

Unlike bisphosphonates which only increase bone density, estrogen uniquely preserves the **flexibility of the inner bone matrix** — the property that prevents catastrophic hip fractures specifically.

### Musculoskeletal Syndrome of Menopause (MSM)

Wright-coined clinical term, peer-reviewed (*Climacteric* 2024). 70% of women experience significant MS symptoms during the menopausal transition; ~25% become functionally disabled. Symptoms: joint pain (arthralgia), rapid lean muscle loss, accelerated bone density loss, rapid osteoarthritis progression, **frozen shoulder** is a frequent MSM presentation that gets misdiagnosed.

### Dose specifics: **Wright withholds**

Wright's clinical posture deliberately refuses universal dose charts in favor of **"Precision Medicine"** — individualization based on baseline labs. Specific patch strengths (0.025 / 0.05 / 0.075 / 0.1 mg), gel doses, vaginal estrogen dose, women's testosterone dose/route — not publicly published. Apply principle; recommend personalization with HRT-literate clinician.

## Hormonal — Men (TRT framework)

Wright's stated position:

- **Critical decade testing 35–45.** Wright recommends asymptomatic baseline T testing in this window: "I have four sons. I'm like, guys, let's go measure it now so we know what to correct to."
- **Individualized peak baseline as TRT target.** Wright's logic: if a man's natural baseline at 35 was 1000 ng/dL, dropping to 600 at 55 (still "normal" by reference range) will manifest as fatigue, injury, ligament fragility. Target the youthful baseline (e.g., 800–1,000 ng/dL).
- TRT-related musculoskeletal protection: testosterone is critical to ligamentous/tendon integrity; Wright traces midlife tendon ruptures and chronic connective-tissue injuries directly to declining T.

### **DIVERGENCE FROM MAINSTREAM (must surface honestly)**

Wright's "critical decade asymptomatic testing" + "youthful peak target" is **more aggressive** than mainstream guidelines:

- **AUA + Endocrine Society** require **symptoms AND** total T <300 ng/dL on two early-morning tests to justify TRT.
- They actively discourage routine asymptomatic screening.
- They target **mid-normal range**, not "youthful peak."

For M users on this skill, present both positions. Asymptomatic + no concerning conditions → Wright's path is one option but not mainstream-supported; symptomatic → both frameworks converge on testing.

### Specifics Wright withholds

Injection routes (IM cypionate vs SC vs enanthate), monitoring cadences, HCG/clomiphene adjunct use — all reserved for her clinical practice, not publicly published.

- **Reject TSH-only testing.** Wright recommends a **comprehensive functional thyroid panel**: TSH, free T3, free T4, reverse T3, antibodies. (Round 2 — Wright platforming Dr. Aaron Hartman.)
- **Optimal not normal.** Standard ranges are dangerously incomplete; the gap between "normal" and "optimal" is where most midlife symptoms live.
- Wright explicitly links suboptimal thyroid to mood instability, memory degradation, cardiac arrhythmias, menstrual disruption.
- **Drug preference (T4 vs T3 vs NDT) not publicly stated.**

## Diagnostics — Wright's extended panel

Wright's "Precision Longevity" clinic uses ~**23 biomarkers** plus genome sequencing. Beyond standard chemistry, this includes:

- **Comite 5 (endorsed via podcast Episode 156):** fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c, **cholesterol/HDL ratio (target <2.0)**, **free testosterone** (Wright: "total testosterone is just about meaningless because it's bound up in protein").
- **Inflammation:** hs-CRP, inflammatory cytokines, total oxidative load, specific interleukins, uric acid.
- **Cellular aging:** intracellular NAD+ (target ~60, midlife depletion can hit 18), senescent cell load (beta-galactosidase blood test or PCR).
- **Epigenetic / biological age:** GlycanAge (glycans on IgG), TruDiagnostic DunedinPACE.
- **Hormonal panel:** beyond Comite 5 — include estradiol/estrone ratio (the menopausal shift from potent estradiol to less potent estrone), FSH, comprehensive thyroid, ferritin, DHEA.
- **Performance baseline:** VO2 max, lactate threshold ("fat max" HR), grip strength, sit-to-stand, DEXA (T-score baseline at age 40).
- **EEG brain mapping** (Nestry helmets — Wright clinic only) — maps brain energy efficiency for cognitive retraining.
- **CGM** (continuous glucose monitor) — Wright uses it personally to track psychological-stress-induced glucose spikes independent of caloric intake.

### Symptom tracking

Wright instructs midlife patients to track: sleep architecture, mood fluctuations, brain fog episodes, menstrual cycle variations, daily energy. This catches early MSM signs and differentiates from "burnout."

### Optimal targets specifically called out by Wright

- Fasting glucose: ~85 mg/dL (110 = prediabetic).
- Cholesterol/HDL ratio: <2.0.
- NAD+ (intracellular): ~60 baseline, declines to ~18.
- VO2 max "frailty floor": **16 (women) / 18 (men)** — below this, independent toilet/chair transition becomes biologically impossible.
- Bone density: DEXA T-score baseline by age 40 (radical departure from standard age 65 timing). Women can lose 20% of bone density in 5–7 years post-menopause.

## Sedentary medicine / NEAT

- **56 hours/week** average sitting time creates pathology. Lipid-metabolism enzymes drop ~50% during prolonged sitting. 4+ hours/day TV → +80% heart-disease mortality.
- Wright's tactical interventions: 10k steps via indirect routes, stability ball instead of desk chair, isometric wall squats during phone calls, rucking.
- **Hourly "vascular widening" breath drill:** maximal inhale, 5-second hold, forceful exhale. Triggers nitric oxide scavengers to clear vascular lipid deposits.

## Frozen shoulder protocol (Wright)

Wright reframes adhesive capsulitis as a glaring inflammatory signal of perimenopausal estrogen depletion — NOT a localized mechanical joint injury. Treatment is **stage-based**, NOT aggressive forced manipulation.

Components: stage-based physical therapy + low-sugar nutrition (cool systemic inflammation) + estradiol + PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) for severe tendon degradation + steroid injection (only to cool the space enough for PT — not as primary treatment) + manipulation under anesthesia as last resort.

Wright's framing quote: "Frozen shoulder demands a stage-based approach; forcing it only backfires."

## Age-tier applicability tags

Wright's framework is primarily built for **midlife** (women 35–55, men 40–60). When applying her corpus to other adult tiers, use these tags:

### Universal (apply across all adult tiers 18+)

- Sleep as #1 pillar (brain recovery, glymphatic clearance) — applies universally.
- F.A.C.E. movement framework — applies; intensity scales by tier.
- 30-30-3 nutrition rule (30 g protein first meal, 30 g fiber, 3 fermented foods) — applies universally.
- 130 g protein / day target, 1.4–2.2 g/kg — applies, but lower end (1.4–1.6 g/kg) sufficient for pre-midlife maintenance; upper end (1.8–2.2 g/kg) for muscle accretion or peri/menopause.
- Anti-fasting stance for women — applies universally (Sims-aligned across reproductive lifespan).
- Caffeine cap (≤400 mg/day, ≤200 mg/sitting) — applies universally.
- Anti-inflammatory food list — applies universally.
- Mindset Mobilization 4-step framework — applies universally.
- Not-To-Do List — applies universally.
- EDC avoidance — applies universally; F users in reproductive years and through perimenopause have highest sensitivity.
- Skincare prevention (retinoid + vit C + azelaic acid + SPF) — applies universally; intensity scales by tier.
- Sauna (Laukkanen) — applies universally where accessible.
- Breathwork (Balban cyclic sighing, Gerbarg resonance) — applies universally.
- Morning light (Czeisler/Panda) — applies universally.
- Foundation supplements (creatine, vit D + K2, magnesium, omega-3, methylated B-complex) — apply universally.

### Pre-midlife (18–34) — SKIP these Wright items

- MSM (Musculoskeletal Syndrome of Menopause) detection — F-only AND midlife+ only.
- HRT framework — F-only AND ≥perimenopause only (typically 40+).
- 10-year HRT window from final menstrual cycle — irrelevant pre-perimenopause.
- Vaginal estrogen / vaginal DHEA — irrelevant pre-perimenopause.
- Testosterone for women — irrelevant pre-perimenopause unless documented deficiency.
- Wright's "critical decade T baseline at 35" — irrelevant for <35 M users.
- Fisetin + quercetin senolytics — senescent cell burden is low at this age; minimal evidence-graded benefit. Skip and note "revisit at midlife."
- NMN / NR — intracellular NAD+ depletion is not the bottleneck at this age. Skip and note "revisit at ~35."
- DEXA at 40 — push earlier ONLY if low BMI + sedentary + zero load-bearing training (peak bone mass window 25–30).
- Wright's full ~23-marker extended panel — overkill; standard panel suffices unless symptomatic.

### Pre-midlife additions (Wright doesn't emphasize but the corpus implies)

- **Bone mass deposit window emphasis** — peak bone mass is reached ~25–30 (per orthopedic literature, Wright's specialty). For users in 18–32 range with low BMI or zero resistance training, the bone-deposit window matters more than midlife withdrawal. Apply heavy resistance + impact loading aggressively.
- **Caloric sufficiency for muscle accretion** — at this tier, body-comp goals typically mean adding lean mass (caloric sufficiency + protein + heavy resistance), not fat loss.
- **HC-specific nutrient depletion handling** — most relevant for this tier (most HC users are pre-midlife). Treat HC formulation specifics seriously (see `availability_by_country.md` HC reference).

### Midlife (35–50 F / 40–55 M) — Wright corpus fully applies

- HRT considerations begin (F).

### Post-midlife (51+ F / 56+ M)

- Wright framework continues with post-menopause / post-andropause emphasis.
- **Frailty floor becomes a primary metric** (VO2max 16 F / 18 M; grip strength; sit-and-rise).
- Bone density loss accelerates — DEXA + resistance + impact loading + HRT (within 10-year window of FMC) are central.
- Mosconi menopause-brain protocol matters most for F (the PET-imaging accelerated-aging window).
- PSA screening shifts (USPSTF Grade C 55–69 M; >70 generally not screened).
- Wright's recommendations on activity preservation (<2%/yr performance decline 50–75 if active vs. >8%/yr after 75) become urgent.
