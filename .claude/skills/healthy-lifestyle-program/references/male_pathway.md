## Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) — Bhasin / AUA / Endocrine Society / Khera / Morgentaler

### Shalender Bhasin, MD (Harvard / Brigham — Endocrine Society Guidelines lead author)

**Financial COI:** Research grants from AbbVie, MIB, FPT; consulting with Novartis. Down-weight for any *therapy-vs-not-therapy* claim; **base assertions are independently corroborated by AUA and TRAVERSE 2023**.

**Protocol (Endocrine Society 2018):**

- TRT is indicated for **symptomatic** men with biochemically confirmed low testosterone — NOT for routine asymptomatic age-related decline.
- For men >65, prescribe TRT **only when consistently and unequivocally** accompanied by clinical symptoms.
- Therapeutic target: **mid-normal serum range**, NOT supraphysiologic "peak youth" levels.
- Monitoring:
  - Serum T at **3–6 months** after initiation, then **yearly** thereafter. Goal: mid-normal range.
  - **Hematocrit** at baseline, 3–6 months, then yearly.
  - **PSA** monitoring per schedule.

Direct Endocrine Society quote: "Monitor T levels 3 to 6 months after initiation of T therapy with a goal level in the mid-normal range. Monitor hematocrit levels at baseline, 3 to 6 months after initiation of T therapy, and then yearly."

### Mohit Khera, MD + Larry Lipshultz, MD (Baylor — AUA Guideline leads)

**Financial COI:** None identified in primary sources regarding direct pharmaceutical compensation for TRT products.

**Protocol (AUA Guideline 2018, updated 2023):**

- **Diagnostic threshold:** Total testosterone <300 ng/dL on **two separate early-morning measurements**, combined with objective clinical symptoms.
- Fasting not required, but testing during acute illness is **forbidden** — illness suppresses T 10–30%.
- Direct AUA quote: "Clinicians should use a total testosterone level below 300 ng/dL as a reasonable cut-off in support of the diagnosis of low testosterone. The diagnosis of low testosterone should be made only after two total testosterone measurements are taken on separate occasions with both conducted in an early morning fashion."

**Fertility-preserving protocol** (for men who want to maintain fertility — standard TRT suppresses HPG axis and halts spermatogenesis):

- **Clomiphene citrate 25 mg every other day** — blocks estrogen negative feedback at the hypothalamus, stimulating endogenous LH and FSH release.
- **hCG 500 IU every other day** — mimics LH, preserves testicular volume and intratesticular testosterone.
- **Estradiol target 30–50 pg/mL** if aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole) is utilized — estrogen remains critical for male libido and bone health; over-suppression is harmful.

### Abraham Morgentaler, MD (Men's Health Boston, Harvard) — saturation model

**Financial COI: EXTENSIVE.** Pharmaceutical relationships with AbbVie, Antares, Auxilium, Bayer, Clarus, Eli Lilly, Endo, Lipocine, Pfizer, TesoRx. Owns commercial men's health clinic (MHB Labs).

**Aggressively down-weight** for safety claims about cardiovascular or prostate risk. Use **only** when independently corroborated.

**Saturation Model of prostate cancer (independently corroborated by retrospective active-surveillance studies):**

Prostate cellular growth is sensitive to androgens at severely depleted (castrate) levels. Once androgen receptors reach maximum binding capacity — which occurs at concentrations **well below normal physiological baseline** — additional exogenous testosterone has **no further proliferative effect**. Therefore, properly monitored TRT in eugonadal-restored range does not induce de novo prostate cancer or accelerate progression in active surveillance.

**Independent corroboration:** TRAVERSE Trial 2023 — 5,204 men (aged 45–80), mean follow-up 33 months. Primary composite endpoint (CV mortality, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke) confirmed **noninferiority** (upper limit of 95% CI for HR <1.5). Notable adverse signals: slightly elevated atrial fibrillation (3.5% vs 2.4%), acute kidney injury (2.3% vs 1.5%), pulmonary embolism (0.9% vs 0.5%).

### TRT key numbers summary

| Metric | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic cut-off | Total T <300 ng/dL on two AM tests | AUA 2023 |
| Therapeutic target | Mid-normal range | Endocrine Society 2018 |
| Estrogen target (if AI used) | 30–50 pg/mL | Khera |
| Hematocrit monitoring | Baseline + 3–6 mo + yearly | Endocrine Society / AUA |
| Fertility-preserving | Clomiphene 25 mg EOD + hCG 500 IU EOD | Khera fertility protocol |

### **DIVERGENCE FROM WRIGHT — surface honestly**

Wright recommends **asymptomatic baseline T testing for men 35–45** AND TRT targeted to **individualized youthful peak** (800–1000 ng/dL).

AUA / Endocrine Society / Bhasin diverge:

1. **Discourage asymptomatic screening** — diagnosis requires symptoms PLUS biochemistry.
2. **Therapeutic target = mid-normal range**, not "peak youth."

Both positions are credible. For asymptomatic M users, present both and let them decide with their clinician. For symptomatic M users with low_t_symptoms, frameworks converge — test.

## Andropause / Male midlife endocrine framework — Travison / Mulhall

### Thomas Travison, PhD (Hebrew SeniorLife / Harvard) — Massachusetts Male Aging Study

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Key finding:** Modifiable lifestyle factors and body composition affect testosterone **as much as or more than** chronological aging.

Direct MMAS quote: "A 4- to 5-kg/m² increase in body mass index or loss of spouse was associated with declines in total serum T comparable to that associated with approximately 10 yr of aging."

**Implication:** Aggressive lifestyle intervention (weight loss, sleep restoration, stress reduction, strength training) must **precede or accompany** any TRT consideration. A 5–10% reduction in body weight is typically required before endogenous T levels show significant recovery.

Validation cohort: 1,667 randomly selected men, Greater Boston, baseline age 40–70, three observation waves over ~15 years.

### John Mulhall, MD (MSKCC) — clinical boundary between hypogonadism and age-related fatigue

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Key principle:** Subjective symptoms alone (fatigue, low mood) are unreliable indicators of testosterone deficiency. Diagnosis requires **objective, systemic signs of androgen deprivation**.

Direct quote: "Symptoms alone are unreliable... objective signs like bone density, A1c, and hematocrit are vital."

**Practical:** Before diagnosing hypogonadism, rule out:

- **Untreated sleep apnea** (treating OSA can normalize T independently of TRT — see Malhotra below).
- **Obesity / metabolic syndrome.**
- **Opioid or steroid use.**
- **Acute illness** (artificially suppresses T 10–30%).

Unexplained high hematocrit can point to undiagnosed OSA rather than primary hypogonadism.

## Prostate health and screening — Brawley / Giovannucci

### Otis Brawley, MD (Johns Hopkins; former CMO American Cancer Society)

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Protocol:** Strict adherence to **USPSTF evidence-based screening paradigms** with shared decision-making, NOT blanket population screening.

USPSTF / AUA-aligned recommendations:

| Age range | Screening | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 55–69 | PSA periodic, **via shared decision-making** with awareness of harms | Grade C |
| ≥70 | **Advised against routine PSA screening** | Grade D |
| Testicular self-exam | Advised against routine clinical screening | Grade D |

**Racial risk stratification (Brawley emphasizes):**

- African American men: 4.2% lifetime risk of prostate cancer death
- White men: 2.3%
- Hispanic men: 2.9%

Risk-stratify screening discussions accordingly.

### Edward Giovannucci, MD, ScD (Harvard School of Public Health) — Health Professionals Follow-Up Study

**Financial COI:** None identified. NIH/NCI-funded.

**Key finding:** Ejaculation frequency is significantly correlated with reduced prostate cancer risk.

- Compared to 4–7 ejaculations per month (lifetime average), men reporting **≥21 ejaculations per month** had a **31% lower risk of prostate cancer** (lifetime average HR 0.66, p-trend < 0.0001).
- Protective effect strongest when behavioral frequency is maintained throughout young adulthood (20–29) AND midlife (40–49).
- Mechanism: prostate gland clearance.

Apply this in the program for M users if they want a lifestyle PCa-prevention recommendation.

## Cardiovascular training — Wisløff (4×4 HIIT) / La Gerche (RV remodeling caution)

### Ulrik Wisløff, PhD (NTNU Trondheim) — Norwegian 4×4 HIIT

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Protocol (Generation 100 study, n=1,500+, 5-yr follow-up):**

| Phase | Duration | Intensity (% HRmax) |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 10 min | 60–70% |
| Interval × 4 | 4 min each | **90–95%** |
| Active recovery × 3 (between intervals) | 3 min each | 60–70% |
| Cooldown | 5 min | 60–70% |
| Total | 38 min | — |

Effect: forces the heart to operate near maximum stroke volume for sustained periods → mitochondrial density adaptations, greater cardiac output, improved muscular oxygen extraction. Wisløff: "Good cardio fitness reduces the risk of over 30 lifestyle diseases as well as premature death by 40 to 50 per cent."

This is the gold-standard CV-longevity HIIT protocol and is compatible with Wright's "2 days/week sprint" framework (use 4×4 as the substantive content of those 2 days, or alternate with Wright's 30-second all-out sprints).

### André La Gerche, MD, PhD (Baker Heart and Diabetes Institute, Melbourne) — RV remodeling

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Caution:** Ultra-endurance training (3–11+ hour events) places disproportionate, prolonged hemodynamic burden on the thin-walled right ventricle. Chronic exposure can lead to maladaptive structural remodeling, cardiac troponin leakage, and **permanent myocardial fibrosis** in a subset of athletes.

CMR imaging study (n=40 well-trained athletes, baseline + post-race + 1-week post-race): "RV volumes increased and all functional measures decreased post-race, whereas LV volumes reduced and function was preserved... Delayed gadolinium enhancement localized to the interventricular septum was identified in 5 of 39 athletes who had greater cumulative exercise exposure."

**Implication for masters M athletes:** moderate-to-intense interval training extends longevity; ultra-endurance loads (marathons + ironman + ultras) carry recognized RV-remodeling risk. The threshold is not precisely quantified, but male masters endurance athletes (who historically dominate ultra cohorts) show **higher rates of atrial fibrillation** as a downstream consequence.

If M user is into ultra-endurance: flag this. Recommend regular CMR/echocardiographic surveillance with a sports cardiologist.

## Resistance training — Phillips (protein dosing) / Schoenfeld (volume × failure)

### Stuart Phillips, PhD (McMaster) — anabolic resistance and per-meal protein

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Anabolic resistance** in aging muscle: older muscles require significantly higher per-meal amino acid concentrations to trigger muscle protein synthesis (MPS) compared to younger muscles.

**Protocol:** "Older adults should prioritize the consumption of **≥0.4 g/kg/meal** — hence **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day with 3–4 feeding occasions** — to increase the frequency of feeding-induced MPS saturation in support of skeletal muscle-related outcomes."

For a 80 kg male: ≥32 g protein per meal, 96–128 g daily across 3–4 meals.

**Critical finding:** in older trial participants, an average supplemental daily protein dose of 20 ± 18 g/day **failed to trigger an anabolic response** — proving that sub-threshold protein boluses are wasted in aging cohorts. Spike doses ARE required.

This compares with Wright's 130 g/day and 20–30 g/meal (slightly higher per-meal but compatible). For midlife M users, prefer Phillips' threshold (0.4 g/kg/meal) as the calibrated minimum, scaling up to Wright's level if user wants to be conservative.

### Brad Schoenfeld, PhD (Lehman College / CUNY) — hypertrophy meta-analyses

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Key principle:** Hypertrophy is primarily a function of **total mechanical volume + proximity to failure**, NOT specific rep ranges. The "hypertrophy zone" (8–12 reps) is a myth.

- "Gains in 1RM strength were significantly greater in favor of high- vs. low-load training... Changes in measures of muscle hypertrophy were similar between conditions."
- For **hypertrophy:** any rep range works if volume is matched and sets are taken near failure.
- For **strength specifically (1RM):** heavy loads (≥80% 1RM) are required.

Cohort: meta-analysis of 21 primary studies, predominantly resistance-trained men.

**Sex applicability:** Schoenfeld's data is M-skewed but the principle applies to F users with one modification — women generally recover faster between sets and can handle slightly higher relative volume at a given intensity due to muscle fiber type distribution (higher type I ratio) + estrogen-mediated muscle repair.

**Implication for the skill:** when M user's goal is hypertrophy, prescribe Wright's 4×4 + 4×8 template but emphasize volume × proximity-to-failure over rep-range orthodoxy. When goal is pure strength → emphasize the heavy 4×4 big-lift block.

## Sleep apnea — Malhotra (severely M-skewed epidemiology)

### Atul Malhotra, MD (UCSD Pulmonary and Sleep Medicine)

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Key finding:** OSA is severely under-managed in male populations. Drives systemic cardiovascular complications AND secondary endocrine failure (including **testosterone suppression**).

**Epidemiology:**

- M:F ratio of OSA in general population: **3:1 to 5:1**; in specific clinical populations: **8:1 to 10:1**.
- Pre-menopausal M: **24% prevalence**; F: **9% prevalence**. (Post-menopause, the gap narrows.)
- Mechanism for M skew: anatomic distribution of male visceral adiposity + upper airway collapsibility.

**Screening:** STOP-BANG questionnaire.

- Score ≥3: **92.4% sensitivity** for OSA (good screening).
- Score ≥5: improves specificity to ~69% — useful when reducing false-positive referrals.

**Weight loss as treatment** (mathematically quantifiable):

> "BMI reduction of 20% was associated with AHI reduction of 57%, while further weight reduction beyond 20% in BMI was associated with a smaller effect on AHI."

→ For obese M with suspected/diagnosed OSA: 20% body-weight reduction → ~57% reduction in apnea-hypopnea index. Weight loss is the highest-leverage intervention; CPAP is the gold-standard treatment for residual disease.

**Crucial clinical link:** treating untreated OSA can normalize testosterone independently of TRT. ALWAYS screen for OSA before considering TRT in M users with low T and any of: snoring, witnessed apnea, daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, BMI ≥30.

**Important sex-difference for F users:** post-menopausal F often present atypically — insomnia, fatigue, morning headaches (NOT loud snoring/gasping). Screen post-menopausal F users with these symptoms for OSA, not just M users.

## Male depression and cognitive aging — Reynolds

### Charles Reynolds III, MD (University of Pittsburgh — geriatric psychiatry)

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Masked male depression framework:**

- Older men present depression atypically: **irritability, anger, externalizing behaviors, somatic complaints, substance use** rather than prototypical tearfulness.
- Failing to recognize these alternative presentations dramatically increases suicide risk.
- **Suicide statistically dominated by older white males using firearms.**

**Practical for skill output:** For M users reporting low_t_symptoms or persistent fatigue / depression / irritability — frame as potentially-masked depression worth professional evaluation, not just "low energy."

**Exercise as primary intervention:**

Meta-analysis of 25 RCTs of exercise interventions for depression in older adults: **large treatment effects** compared to control conditions.

- Mechanism: physical activity acts as a multi-pathway intervention restoring **hippocampal monoamines** (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine) and preserving cerebral blood flow.
- Reynolds: "Physical activity is an effective component of depression management... The serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine levels in the hippocampi were significantly increased by exercise."

**Cognitive-aging sex difference:**

- F cognitive aging: driven by **abrupt estrogen loss** in menopause (sharp).
- M cognitive aging: driven by **continuous vascular/metabolic degradation** + lifelong testosterone effects on cerebrovasculature (gradual).

→ For M users: emphasize **vascular health and metabolic control** as the primary cognitive-preservation levers (overlap with cardio + nutrition pillars).

## Wim Hof method — Kox (M-cohort safety data)

### Matthijs Kox, PhD (Radboud University Medical Centre — independent academic evaluator)

**Financial COI:** None — independent academic researcher, not affiliated with Wim Hof commercial enterprise.

**Protocol** (10-day training): cyclic hyperventilation + breath retention + cold exposure.

**Primary trial (PNAS 2014):** n=12 healthy young male volunteers; experimental human endotoxemia (LPS injection) to measure immune modulation.

**Demonstrated physiological effects:**

- Acute spike in epinephrine.
- Upregulates anti-inflammatory IL-10.
- Suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine cascades (e.g., TNF-α) during acute inflammatory challenge.

Kox quote: "The findings suggest that the WHM may reduce inflammation in healthy and non-healthy participants as it increases epinephrine levels, causing an increase in interleukin-10 and a decrease in pro-inflammatory cytokines."

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. Strong mechanism, narrow validation cohort (n=12, young males only). Specific 10-day training-then-LPS-challenge is not the same as routine practice in the general population.

**Apply for M users ONLY** — Wim Hof method has not been validated in female cohorts; F immune systems are generally more reactive and prone to autoimmunity, and the sympathetic/epinephrine-spike mechanism interacts unfavorably with perimenopausal autonomic dysregulation (see Sims in `female_pathway.md`).

**Contraindications (general):** history of cardiac arrhythmia, severe hypertension, pregnancy. Skill should not push WHM on M users with cardiovascular issues without clinician oversight.

   - **Diagnostics**: PSA per USPSTF Grade C (55–69) shared decision-making; advised against ≥70.

   - **Movement / strength**: Wright 4×4 + 4×8 + Schoenfeld volume principle + Phillips per-meal protein floor (0.4 g/kg/meal).
   - **Sleep pillar**: aggressive sleep-apnea screening (STOP-BANG) given M epidemiology.
   - **Cold/breathwork**: Wim Hof full protocol available; extreme cold tolerable per Soeberg M cohort.
   - **Mental health**: Reynolds masked-male-depression framework if low_t_symptoms / irritability / persistent fatigue.
   - **Prostate**: Giovannucci ejaculation-frequency finding as a lifestyle PCa-prevention recommendation.

### M user with low_t_symptoms

Before recommending TRT discussion:

1. **Screen for sleep apnea first** (Malhotra). Treating OSA can normalize T independently.
2. **Lifestyle intervention floor** per Travison (5–10% body weight reduction expected to recover endogenous T).
3. **Then** discuss the TRT framework with AUA/Endocrine Society as the mainstream path AND Wright's more aggressive path.

### M user asymptomatic 35–45

This is the **divergence** case. Wright says test now; AUA/Endocrine Society discourage screening asymptomatic. Skill should:

- Present both positions.
- Frame as a values-based decision: more aggressive proactive testing (Wright) vs. mainstream "test when symptomatic" (AUA).
- Note that lifestyle interventions (sleep, weight, strength training, EDC avoidance) apply equally regardless of which testing path is chosen.
