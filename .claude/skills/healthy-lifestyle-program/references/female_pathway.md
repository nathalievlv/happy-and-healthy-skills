## Stacy Sims, PhD — female-specific training and nutrition

**Active role:** Exercise physiologist and nutrition scientist; former research director at Stanford and AUT University. Authority on sex differences in training/health.

**Financial COI:** Very low. Sells female-focused educational micro-courses via her website; no supplement line or device equity.

**Foundational premise:** "Women are not smaller men." Protocols derived from male cohorts (especially fasting, extreme cold, low-carb peri-workout) frequently fail or backfire in women.

### Macronutrient timing (F-specific)

- **Abandon fasted training.** Calorie restriction, delayed breakfast, and fasted workouts trigger a massive stress response in the female body — drives cortisol up, drains energy, suppresses thyroid, paradoxically increases fat storage.
- **Pre-workout:** women must consume **a minimum of 15 g protein** (e.g., whey or plant protein in almond milk) **prior to lifting weights**. For endurance training: add **~30 g carbohydrates**.
- **Post-workout (anabolic window):**
  - **Reproductive-age women:** **35 g protein** within 45 minutes of finishing the workout.
  - **Peri/menopause women:** **40–60 g protein** within 45 minutes — required to overcome the age- and estrogen-decline-associated anabolic resistance that prevents muscle protein synthesis.
- **Daily target:** **~1 g protein per pound of body weight** (~2.2 g/kg). Bulk of daily carbs from fruits and vegetables; reserve starchy carbohydrates for the pre- or post-workout metabolic window.

Source: ROAR, Next Level, clinical newsletters; BMJ Open Sport Exerc Med (2021).

### Cycle-synced HRV interpretation (F-only)

HRV (heart rate variability) reflects autonomic balance. In naturally cycling women, **estrogen increases vagal tone** (raises HRV); **progesterone decreases vagal tone** and stimulates sympathetic activity.

- HRV is highest in the low-hormone (**follicular**) phase — high physiological resilience.
- After ovulation, rising progesterone suppresses HRV and increases resting heart rate.
- **Critical:** consumer-wearable recovery metrics will organically drop during the luteal phase, regardless of actual muscular recovery. Cycling women must **track their cycle alongside biometrics** and **not blindly obey algorithm-generated "red" recovery days** during the luteal phase if subjective energy and sleep quality are otherwise good.

Sex difference: male HRV baselines lack monthly steroidal fluctuations, making male-trained wearable algorithms inaccurate for cycling female physiology.

### Cold immersion — F-specific temperature modification

Sims explicitly warns women — particularly perimenopausal women — against ice-cold (<10 °C) plunges:

- For women, the physiological benefits of cold exposure (reduced systemic inflammation, BAT activation) come at **10–15 °C (50–59 °F)** — sufficient without overstressing the system.
- Extreme cold (<10 °C) is a massive sympathetic stressor that can trigger **chronic cortisol elevation**, especially in perimenopausal women whose autonomic nervous systems are already burdened.
- Male-derived "ice plunge" protocols (Huberman, Wim Hof popular content) can trap women in sympathetic overdrive.

**Apply:** F users get cold immersion ONLY at 10–15 °C, not colder. (Pair with Soeberg's BAT activation framework — see sex_neutral.md.)

## Lisa Mosconi, PhD — menopause-brain protocol

**Active role:** Neuroscientist; Director of the Alzheimer's Prevention Program and the Women's Brain Initiative at Weill Cornell Medicine. Active academic appointment with grant-funded research lab.

**Financial COI:** None identified beyond standard book royalties.

### The menopause-brain framework

Through advanced PET neuroimaging, Mosconi demonstrated that the **risk of Alzheimer's accelerates drastically during the menopausal transition** because the systemic decline in estrogen deprives the female brain of vital neuroprotective energy and glucose metabolism. Estrogen acts as a master regulator of brain energy metabolism in women; its loss is the central mechanism behind women representing **nearly two-thirds of all Alzheimer's cases**.

Quote: "By doing brain scans, we have shown that the brain of a 60-year-old woman on the Mediterranean diet looks five years younger than that of a 50-year-old woman on the Western diet."

### Brain-optimized Mediterranean diet

To counter the energetic crisis:

- Antioxidant berries, nuts, seeds.
- Extra virgin olive oil.
- Fatty fish (omega-3 for membranes).
- Whole grains.
- Greens and vegetables.

### Breakfast Protocol (Mosconi)

- The brain requires **light, sustained energy upon waking**.
- Breakfast must be **glucose-rich, high-fiber, with adequate protein and hydration**.
- **Avoid** heavy, high-fat, or high-sugar meals — these crash cognition.
- Examples Mosconi endorses: Bircher muesli, avocado toast.

Sources: *The Menopause Brain*, *Brain Food*. Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience publications.

## Felice Gersh, MD — women's hormones and EDC framework

**Active role:** OB/GYN; founder/director of the Integrative Medical Group of Irvine; former Assistant Clinical Professor of OB/GYN at the Keck USC School of Medicine.

**Financial COI:** Operates a clinic that sells aesthetics, skincare, and targeted testing panels. COI exists, but her EDC avoidance recommendations are derived from the primary toxicology literature, not her product lines.

### EDC avoidance for female hormonal optimization

Endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) interact directly with the female hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, accelerating reproductive aging, promoting premature ovarian failure through accelerated follicle depletion, and diminishing circulating estradiol.

Gersh prioritizes EDC elimination for women managing **PCOS** and women in the **menopausal transition**, since environmental toxins exacerbate the inflammatory and metabolic burdens already present.

Specific EDC classes to avoid (from primary toxicology, Gersh-endorsed):

- **Phthalates** (in fragrance, plastic, soft PVC)
- **Bisphenols** (BPA, BPS, BPF — in plastics, food can linings, thermal receipts)
- **Parabens** (methyl-, propyl-, butyl- — preservatives in skincare)
- **PFAS / "forever chemicals"** (waterproof coatings, non-stick cookware, some cosmetics)
- **Triclosan** (antibacterial agents)
- **Oxybenzone, benzophenone-3** (chemical sunscreens)
- **Synthetic fragrance / parfum** (frequent phthalate carrier)
- **Formaldehyde-releasers** (DMDM hydantoin, quaternium-15)

Mechanism: structural mimicry of natural steroid hormones; act as **estrogen receptor agonists** and **androgen receptor antagonists**. Phthalates also modulate inflammatory pathways and induce oxidative stress through nuclear receptor interaction.

## Sara Gottfried, MD — cortisol-conscious nutrition

**Active role:** Harvard-trained physician; women's hormonal health; books *The Hormone Cure*, *Women, Food, and Hormones*.

**Financial COI: MODERATE.** Sells commercial diet programs and proprietary supplement lines. Cite specifically for the **mechanism** of cortisol-driven visceral adiposity (an established endocrine fact, independently verifiable) — not for product or program recommendations.

### Cortisol-conscious nutrition framework

Chronic stress overrides all other hormonal signaling through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal-thyroid-gonadal (HPATG) axis. Chronically elevated cortisol → raised blood sugar → insulin resistance → **accumulation of visceral belly fat**.

Mechanism: **abdominal fat cells possess four times more cortisol receptors than fat cells elsewhere in the body.** This is the established endocrine pathway that justifies stress-management AND macronutrient timing as anti-visceral-adiposity interventions, especially in midlife women whose HPA axis is increasingly dysregulated.

Practical translation: macronutrient timing must stabilize blood sugar to prevent cortisol spikes. Sims' anti-fasted-training rule applies. Late-day high-glycemic meals worsen the loop.

### F-user pregnant, trying to conceive, breastfeeding, or recently postpartum

Use the top-level `pregnancy_status` field (added in schema v1.1) — `currently_pregnant`, `breastfeeding`, `trying_to_conceive`, or `recently_postpartum (<12 months)`. Each status triggers an automatic gate the skill applies before producing the program.

**Hard exclusions — do NOT recommend any of these:**

- All retinoids — topical and oral. Vitamin A derivatives are teratogenic. Stop topical retinoids 1 month before trying to conceive.
- Senolytics (fisetin, quercetin) — no human pregnancy safety data.
- NMN / NR — no human pregnancy safety data.
- Extended fasting / TRE longer than 12 hours — disrupts maternal glycemic control + fetal nutrient supply.
- Sauna ≥80 °C in first trimester especially; second/third trimester proceed only with clinician clearance.
- Cold immersion below room temperature — vasoconstriction concerns; not validated in pregnancy cohorts.
- Wim Hof method (cyclic hyperventilation) — induces hypocapnia, vasoconstriction; not pregnancy-validated.
- Alcohol — any amount.
- Oral DHEA, off-label testosterone, compounded hormones — not validated in pregnancy.
- High-dose unverified supplements — stick to verified pregnancy-safe products (prenatal vitamin, vitamin D, choline, omega-3 with low mercury).

**Automatic adjustments:**

- **Vitamin D dose:** Hollis 4,000–6,000 IU/day during pregnancy and lactation (vs. 2,000 IU baseline).
- **Calcium emphasis:** 1,000–1,200 mg/day from food + supplement gap-fill.
- **Choline:** 450 mg/day pregnant, 550 mg/day lactating (often missing from prenatals).
- **Iron:** priority. Ferritin <50 ng/mL in pregnancy is functional deficiency. Verify with prenatal labs.
- **Omega-3 (EPA + DHA):** 1–2 g/day, with DHA ≥200 mg/day for fetal brain development. IFOS-certified for low mercury.
- **Protein:** maintain 1.4–1.6 g/kg/day; do not exceed unless clinician-directed.
- **Movement:** moderate resistance + brisk walking + prenatal yoga remain appropriate. Heavy 1RM lifts and Valsalva-heavy moves typically de-emphasized after first trimester.

**Skill output adjustments:**

- Add a "Pregnancy-aware notes" header at the top of the program output, listing what's been excluded/adjusted and why.
- Direct user to a prenatal-aware clinician (OB/GYN or midwife) for hormone-touching pillars and any clinical decisions.
- For `trying_to_conceive`: most exclusions still apply preemptively (1-month retinoid washout, supplement screening).
- For `breastfeeding`: most pregnancy exclusions persist; protein 1.6–1.8 g/kg/day (Sims breastfeeding target).
- For `recently_postpartum <12 months`: many exclusions persist if breastfeeding; otherwise gradually return to standard program over months 6–12.

### F-user on hormonal contraception (any method)

Use the top-level `hormonal_contraception_method` field (schema v1.1). Do NOT treat "on HC" as a single category — adjust per the specific brand/method per `availability_by_country.md` "Hormonal contraceptive brand reference."

Key adjustments by method class:

- **Combined oral pills (Yaz, Microgynon, Diane-35, etc.):** highest hepatic impact → highest B-vitamin / magnesium / zinc depletion. Methylated B-complex is most consequential here. SHBG ↑ significantly → free testosterone ↓.
- **Vaginal ring (Ornibel / NuvaRing) + transdermal patch:** lower hepatic impact → reduced (not eliminated) depletion. SHBG ↑ moderately.
- **Hormonal IUD (Mirena / Kyleena):** minimal systemic absorption → minimal depletion / SHBG impact.
- **Copper IUD:** no hormonal impact; the program treats user as if they had no HC except contraceptive method is mentioned.
- **Depot progestin (DMPA / Depo-Provera):** **documented bone-density loss** (FDA black-box). Surface this honestly. DEXA earlier than usual; calcium + vitamin D + K2 + heavy resistance training emphasized; have the user discuss DMPA-alternative with gynecologist if bone-density risk factors stack.

Apply Sims' cycle-synced HRV framing ONLY for women not on HC suppression — ring/patch/IUD-progestin/DMPA users have HC-driven hormone patterns, not endogenous cycle. Wearable "luteal-phase HRV drop" still occurs in some users on combined methods during the ring-free / pill-free week (controlled withdrawal bleed) but reflects HC pattern, not natural cycle.
