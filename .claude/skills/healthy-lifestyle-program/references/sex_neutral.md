## Sauna — Jari Laukkanen, MD, PhD (KIHD study, University of Eastern Finland)

**Financial COI:** None identified. Operates strictly in academic and clinical capacities.

**Protocol — traditional Finnish sauna (NOT low-temp infrared):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Ambient temperature | **80–100 °C (174–212 °F)** at face level |
| Humidity | 10–20% |
| Session duration | **≥19 minutes** for optimal cardioprotective effect |
| Frequency | **4–7 sessions/week** for optimal mortality reduction |

**Mortality data (independently validated in both sexes):**

- 2015 JAMA Internal Medicine study, n=2,315 middle-aged men, 20+ year follow-up: men with 4–7 sauna sessions/week had **HR 0.37 (95% CI 0.18–0.75)** for sudden cardiac death vs. one session/week.
- 2018 BMC Medicine follow-up, n=1,688 middle-aged-to-elderly, 51% female: **same inverse association** with fatal CV events in women.

→ Female-cohort validated. Apply to both sexes.

**Mechanism:** mimics moderate-intensity exercise — elevated heart rate, improved vascular compliance, lower systemic blood pressure.

**Divergence on infrared:** Infrared saunas (45–60 °C) provide subjective relaxation but **the hard mortality-benefit data is for traditional Finnish sauna ≥80 °C only**. Don't conflate the two. Country-availability: traditional Finnish sauna access varies — see `availability_by_country.md`.

**Contraindications:** uncontrolled severe hypertension, recent MI, pregnancy (especially early), acute illness.

## Cold exposure — Susanna Soeberg, PhD (Soeberg Institute, Copenhagen)

**Financial COI:** Founder of commercial Soeberg Institute (sells certification programs, books). Cite the underlying physiology from her peer-reviewed Cell Reports Medicine (2021) paper; flag commercial framing.

**Protocol — "Soeberg Principle":**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Total weekly cold immersion | **~11 minutes per week** |
| Sessions | **2–3 sessions/week** |
| Critical: end the cycle on **cold** (not warm) | Forces the body to rewarm itself, recruiting brown adipose tissue (BAT) and shivering |
| Pair with heat | ~57 minutes of weekly heat exposure (sauna) |

**Mechanism:** activates BAT thermogenesis, increases caloric expenditure, enhances systemic fat metabolism. Shivering releases molecules that trigger thermogenesis in brown fat.

**Cohort caveat:** The 2021 Cell Reports Medicine validation study was **experienced winter-swimming men**. Female circadian temperature regulation differs across the menstrual cycle.

### Sex-specific temperature thresholds

- **For M users:** Soeberg's underlying M-cohort tolerated extreme cold (winter swimming). Standard cold-plunge protocols (10 °C or lower, 1–3 min) align.
- **For F users:** override with Sims' modification (in `female_pathway.md`) — **10–15 °C, never below 10 °C**. Extreme cold can trigger chronic cortisol elevation in perimenopausal women.

**Contraindications:** cardiovascular disease (afterdrop risk), Raynaud's, pregnancy, severe hypertension. Cold shower is a fine universal substitute for those without plunge access.

## Morning light — Satchin Panda, PhD + Charles Czeisler, MD, PhD

### Satchin Panda, PhD (Salk Institute, Regulatory Biology Laboratory)

**Financial COI:** None identified. Grant-funded academic.

**Protocol:**

- Spend **30–60 minutes outdoors** with eyes exposed to daylight soon after waking.
- Even on a cloudy day OR while sitting in a car looking horizontally, eyes receive **5,000–10,000 lux** — the threshold to trigger blue-light-sensing melanopsin receptors in the retina.
- Mechanism: synchronizes the brain's master circadian clock → prevents afternoon sleepiness, regulates mood, governs nightly melatonin release.

### Charles Czeisler, MD, PhD (Brigham and Women's / Harvard — primary investigator on human circadian physiology for 40+ years)

**Financial COI:** None identified.

**Protocol — quantitative lux thresholds:**

- **~10,000 lux** of bright white light (equivalent to ambient outdoor just after sunrise) significantly reduces plasma cortisol during the rising and descending phases of the circadian rhythm.
- **Typical dim indoor light (1.5–3 lux)** is functionally interpreted as darkness — virtually no entrainment effect.
- **Light therapy lamps emitting 10,000 lux** can substitute for outdoor light when environmental conditions prohibit it (winter, high latitude, no outdoor access).

Both researchers represent the absolute vanguard of circadian science. Agreement: **morning light is the supreme zeitgeber for sleep/wake regulation and acute cortisol suppression.** Standard indoor lighting is completely insufficient.

Czeisler's lab is also studying light exposure as a countermeasure for **menstrual phase-dependent neurobehavioral performance** in women — F users with cycle-related mood/cognition shifts may benefit from extra morning-light exposure.

**Apply to all users.** Constraint flag: `constraints.no_outdoor_access=true` → recommend 10,000-lux therapy lamp substitute. `constraints.shift_work_or_irregular_schedule=true` → modified entrainment guidance (consult sleep specialist).

## Vitamin D — Heike Bischoff-Ferrari, MD, DrPH + Bruce Hollis, PhD

### Heike Bischoff-Ferrari (University Hospital Zurich — DO-HEALTH trial lead)

**Financial COI:** None identified. Funded by large academic grants.

**Protocol (DO-HEALTH trial — multicenter European RCT, n=2,157, 61.7% female, healthy older adults):**

| Daily intervention | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Vitamin D3** | **2,000 IU** |
| **Marine omega-3** | **1 g** |
| **Strength exercise (SHEP — Simple Home Exercise Program)** | Daily home program |

Effect: combined triad significantly reduces invasive cancer risk and supports cognitive + musculoskeletal health.

**CRITICAL warning:** Do **NOT** use **high-dose monthly boluses** (e.g., 60,000 IU once a month). The trial showed monthly boluses provided no benefit to lower extremity function and **paradoxically increased fall risk and incidence** in older adults vs. steady daily dosing (24,000 IU/month ≈ 800 IU/day). **Daily dosing is mandatory.**

### Bruce Hollis (MUSC — vitamin D pharmacokinetics)

**Financial COI:** None identified. Primary lab/clinical researcher.

**Protocol — optimal serum target and supplementation math:**

- **Target serum 25(OH)D ≥ 100 nmol/L (≥40 ng/mL).**
- Standard adult recommendations (200–600 IU/day) are mathematically incapable of maintaining sufficiency.
- Adults and pregnant women often require **4,000–6,000 IU/day** D3 to reach target.
- Higher dose (4,000–6,000 IU) explicitly required during **pregnancy, lactation, and postmenopause** to offset skeletal demineralization.

### Endogenous synthesis via sun (Hollis protocol)

If using sun exposure:

- Expose **arms, legs, and torso** to sunlight for **25–50% of the time** it would take to develop a mild sunburn (= 1 Minimal Erythemal Dose, or MED).
- **2–3 times per week.**
- Close to **solar noon (10:00–16:00)** for maximum UV-B.

**Skin-type scaling (Fitzpatrick):** Type VI skin requires roughly **5× the duration of solar exposure** compared to Type I to synthesize 1,000 IU of D3.

Use `climate_location.skin_type_fitzpatrick` and `climate_location.latitude_band` to calibrate. **High-latitude users (above 55°)** cannot synthesize meaningful vit D from sun in winter — default to supplementation.

### Convergence

Both researchers agree on the optimal target serum range. Bischoff-Ferrari focuses on **daily supplementation** in RCT context. Hollis provides the pharmacokinetic math for sun vs. supplementation. **Use Bischoff-Ferrari's DO-HEALTH triad as the default supplementation protocol; layer in Hollis's sun math when the user has outdoor access and Fitzpatrick-appropriate skin.**

**Holick exclusion:** Michael Holick is widely cited on vitamin D but had a documented financial COI with the indoor tanning industry (NYT 2018). His primary research is cited where unavoidable, but his protocol-level recommendations on sun exposure are excluded from this corpus.

## Breathwork — Balban/Spiegel (cyclic sighing) + Gerbarg/Brown (resonance breathing)

### Melis Yilmaz Balban, PhD + David Spiegel, MD (Stanford Medicine)

**Financial COI:** Primary investigators carry no relevant commercial COI. Their co-author Andrew Huberman has extensive commercial partnerships — bypass Huberman and cite the primary research directly.

**Protocol — "Cyclic Sighing" for acute stress mitigation:**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Inhale deeply through the nose |
| 2 | Take a **second, smaller inhale** to fully expand the pulmonary alveoli |
| 3 | Execute a **long, slow exhale completely through the mouth** |

Duration: **5 minutes daily.**

Effect: cyclic sighing produces **significantly greater increases in positive affect and reductions in physiological arousal** compared to mindfulness meditation. Triggers vagal-tone elevation via prolonged exhalation, downregulates sympathetic activity.

**Source:** Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine (2023). Randomized controlled trial, n=111.

**Sex-applicability:** No specific female-modifications needed. Cohort was mixed. **Apply to both sexes.**

### Patricia Gerbarg, MD (New York Medical College) + Richard Brown, MD (Columbia)

**Financial COI:** Both researchers receive remuneration for teaching their "Breath-Body-Mind" branded programs. The **biomechanics of resonance breathing** are independently validated by broad psychophysiological literature — cite the biomechanics, not their branded course.

**Protocol — Resonance / Coherent Breathing:**

Breathe at **~5.5 breaths per minute**:

- **5.5 second inhale**
- **5.5 second exhale**

Effect: creates a resonant frequency within the cardiovascular system → maximizes HRV → optimizes sympathovagal balance → enhances parasympathetic vagal tone.

**Source:** Gerbarg/Brown, OBM Integrative and Complementary Medicine (2019). Independent psychophysiology corroboration broad and consistent.

**Apply to both sexes.**

### When to use which

- **Cyclic sighing**: acute stress, sleep preparation, immediate physiological arousal reduction. Quick win.
- **Resonance breathing**: HRV training, longer practice for autonomic tone over time.

Wim Hof method (cyclic hyperventilation) is a **separate** breathwork class — up-regulating, NOT down-regulating. Apply only to M users (see `male_pathway.md`).

## HRV monitoring — independent validation (Dial et al. 2025)

**Note:** We deliberately bypass Whoop and HRV4Training researchers (Holmes, Altini) due to commercial COI on hardware. Cite the **independent academic validation**.

**Protocol — hardware accuracy:**

- **Finger-worn optical sensors (e.g., Oura Ring)** demonstrate superior accuracy over wrist-worn for nocturnal HRV measurement due to closer arterial proximity and reduced motion artifacts.
- Independent validation (Dial et al., The Physiological Society 2025, 536 nights):
  - **Oura Ring 4**: concordance correlation coefficient **CCC = 0.99** vs. ECG reference.
  - **WHOOP 5.0**: CCC = 0.94 (still strong but lower).

**Interpretation (Sims' female framework — see `female_pathway.md` for F users):** in cycling F, HRV drops in the luteal phase are normal physiology, not under-recovery.

**Apply HRV monitoring** if user `preferences.biometric_tracking_comfort` is high or medium. Skip if low — recommend subjective tracking instead.

## Mobility — Kelly Starrett, DPT + Stuart McGill, PhD

### Kelly Starrett, DPT (The Ready State, "Built to Move")

**Financial COI:** Operates a commercial enterprise selling mobility tools, paid subscription apps, and books. Cite biomechanical movement **principles**; flag product-specific recommendations.

**Protocol — 10 essential habits / 4 key equipment-free interventions:**

1. **Sit-and-Rise Test:** ability to sit cross-legged on the floor and stand back up without using hands, knees, or external support. Tests fundamental hip mobility and motor control. Independently validated as a predictor of all-cause mortality in aging populations.
2. **Daily steps:** baseline **8,000–10,000 steps**.
3. **Deep squatting:** ability to lower into a deep squat and hold the position comfortably. Preserves ankle dorsiflexion and hip flexion.
4. **The Morning Spin-Up:** dedicated **10-minute morning routine** — breathing expansion + lower-body soft tissue mobilization to prepare joints for daily load.

Apply to both sexes; foundational mobility.

### Stuart McGill, PhD (University of Waterloo, emeritus) — McGill Big 3

**Financial COI:** Sells clinical consulting services, educational courses, books via BackFitPro. Cite for biomechanical isometric principles (independently validated).

**Protocol — "The McGill Big 3" core stabilization:**

Designed to build muscular endurance and protect the lumbar spine from undue shear/compressive stress. Explicitly **forbids dynamic spinal flexion** (no traditional sit-ups or crunches).

| Exercise | Setup |
|---|---|
| **McGill Curl-up** | Hands under lumbar spine to preserve lordotic arch; one leg straight, one bent; elevate head and shoulders as a single rigid unit |
| **Side Plank / Side Bridge** | Lift hips from side-lying position; long neck, rigidly braced core |
| **Bird Dog** | From quadruped: simultaneously extend opposite arm and leg; perfectly neutral, unmoving spine |

**Rep scheme:** Reverse pyramid (e.g., 5 reps → 3 reps → 1 rep). Each isometric position held **8–10 seconds**. Holding longer induces muscle hypoxia + form breakdown.

McGill's biomechanical position is also: **avoid yoga poses that involve end-range spinal flexion under load.**

Apply to both sexes — spinal architecture is mechanically uniform across sexes per McGill's published EMG and biomechanical data.

### Wendy Suzuki, PhD (NYU — Professor of Neural Science and Psychology)

**Financial COI:** None identified beyond book royalties.

**Protocol — exercise-induced neurogenesis:**

  - Rapidly elevates **Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)**.
  - Elevates **Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1)**.

## Time-restricted eating — Satchin Panda, PhD (sex-divergent outcomes)

(Already cited above for morning light. Here for TRE.)

**Protocol — TRE 10–12 hour window:**

- Limit all daily food intake to a consistent **10- to 12-hour window** (e.g., 8 AM – 6 PM).
- Aligns nutrient ingestion with circadian rhythms.
- Improves sleep architecture, prevents metabolic disease, reduces liver fat accumulation.

**Source:** Cell Reports (2021); human clinical trials + pre-clinical mouse models.

**SEX-DIVERGENT outcomes (must apply):**

- **M cohorts:** robust protection from weight gain under TRE.
- **F cohorts:** NOT protected from weight gain to the same degree. BUT female subjects still achieve metabolic benefits (whole-body glucose regulation, protection against fatty liver disease and glucose intolerance) — independent of weight change.

**Apply:** M users can use TRE for weight + metabolic benefits. F users get metabolic benefits without weight loss — and should be informed of this distinction. **Pregnant or breastfeeding women: do NOT practice TRE.**

This integrates with Wright's anti-extended-fasting stance for F users. 10–12 hour TRE is moderate and Wright-compatible if user wants it; longer fasts are not recommended for F users per Wright.

## EDC primary toxicology (sex-applicable, sex-divergent harm)

(Detail in `female_pathway.md` under Gersh/Haver. Here is the universal primary-toxicology layer.)

EDCs to aggressively avoid in personal-care products (the universal avoid-list from peer-reviewed toxicology — independent of any clinician personality):

1. **Phthalates** (DEHP, DBP, BBzP) — plasticizers, fragrance carriers.
2. **Bisphenols** (BPA, BPS, BPF) — plastic linings, thermal receipts, food can interiors.
3. **Parabens** — especially methylparaben (skincare preservative); also propyl-, butyl-, ethyl-.
4. **PFAS / "forever chemicals"** — non-stick coatings, waterproof cosmetics, dental floss.
5. **Triclosan** — antibacterial agents in soap/hand sanitizer.
6. **Oxybenzone, benzophenone-3** — chemical sunscreens.
7. **Synthetic fragrance / parfum** — common phthalate carrier.
8. **Formaldehyde-releasers** (DMDM hydantoin, quaternium-15) — preservatives.

**Mechanism:** structural mimicry of natural steroid hormones; estrogen receptor agonists + androgen receptor antagonists. Phthalates additionally modulate inflammatory pathways and induce oxidative stress through nuclear receptor interaction.

**Sex-divergent harm severity:** F vulnerability is documented — accelerated reproductive aging, premature ovarian failure, diminished circulating estradiol, predictive marker for female-specific hypertriglyceridemia and metabolic syndrome years post-exposure. M vulnerability includes declining testosterone (popularly cited but evidence varies in strength).

**Practical:** apply EDC avoidance to all users; emphasize for F especially during reproductive years and the menopausal transition. **Reference lists like EWG Skin Deep** can identify clean products by ingredient; this is sex-neutral guidance grounded in primary toxicology, not in any one clinician's product recommendation.

## Iron supplementation — Stoffel et al. every-other-day evidence

Across all iron formulations (sulfate, bisglycinate, gluconate, polymaltose) the standard advice of "take iron daily" is being overturned by recent absorption-physiology research.

**Key finding (Stoffel et al., Lancet Haematology 2017; replication 2020):**

- A single iron dose elevates serum **hepcidin** (the master iron-regulating hormone) for ≥24 hours.
- Elevated hepcidin blocks intestinal absorption of subsequent iron doses via ferroportin downregulation.
- **Daily dosing reduces fractional absorption by ~50%** compared to every-other-day dosing.
- **Every-other-day dosing absorbs 2–3× more total iron** per mg supplemented over time.

**Implications for the program:**

- For users with **ferritin <30 ng/mL** (functional deficiency): daily may still be appropriate for rapid repletion if tolerated.
- For users with **ferritin 30–80 ng/mL** (moving up, working toward target 50–80): every-other-day is often superior.
- For users with **ferritin 80–120 ng/mL** (at or near target): step down to maintenance (smaller dose every-other-day or 3×/week).
- For users with **ferritin >150 ng/mL** while still supplementing: pause and retest in 3 months — iron overload risk is real.

Apply this to the iron pillar of the diagnostics calibration table.

## Iron + zinc absorption competition (DMT1)

A common but under-flagged absorption interaction: **iron and zinc both use divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1)** in the intestine. Co-administration reduces absorption of both.

**Practical rules:**

- Separate iron and zinc by at least 2–3 hours.
- Standard scheduling: iron in the morning (empty stomach + vitamin C source — 250 mg tablet, half a kiwi, or a few strawberries; **not fruit juice**, which delivers a glucose spike on an empty stomach), zinc in the afternoon (with food), magnesium at night.
- Coffee, tea, calcium-rich foods, dairy, phytate-rich foods (oats, whole grains, legumes), and polyphenol-rich foods (berries, dark chocolate) all block iron absorption — keep iron away from these by ≥30–60 minutes. A common real-world failure: taking iron after a morning smoothie/oatmeal that contains multiple absorption blockers, leading to years of stalled ferritin despite daily supplementation.

The skill should always output this timing logic when prescribing iron or zinc.

## Reformer pilates is NOT a substitute for heavy resistance training

Active pilates practitioners frequently conflate reformer pilates with the resistance training Wright/Schoenfeld/Phillips prescribe. The skill must address this head-on rather than assuming the user understands the distinction.

**What reformer pilates does well (✓):**

- Core, hip stability, postural endurance, end-range control, scapular health.
- Spring resistance (~5–35 kg carriage equivalent) provides real strength stimulus.
- Excellent complement to heavy lifting.
- Good for sensitive backs / rehabilitation contexts.

**What reformer pilates does NOT do well vs. free weights (✗):**

- **Bone density (osteogenic stimulus):** weak. Bone responds to high-magnitude axial compressive loading (barbell on spine, deadlift pulling vertically through the skeleton, jumping impact). Reformer largely keeps the user lying or seated with horizontal force vectors. Wolff's law applies axially.
- **Maximal strength:** hard to express true 1RM strength on a reformer.
- **Hypertrophy / lean mass accretion:** load × volume × proximity-to-failure is lower than free weights.

**For the program:** if the user is doing reformer pilates and asks whether they need additional resistance training, the answer is yes for any of these goals: bone density, peak strength, lean mass, body recomposition. Keep one or two pilates classes for complementary spinal stability; add 2–3 heavy free-weight sessions per week.

## Tinted SPF and visible-light protection (HEV / blue light)

For users with melasma risk factors (hormonal contraception + Fitzpatrick II–IV + sunny climate), or who already have visible hyperpigmentation, **proactively recommend tinted SPF** rather than waiting for the user to ask.

**Mechanism distinction:**

- **Melanoma is overwhelmingly UV-driven** (UVA + UVB). UV protection comes from the SPF filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, Mexoryl, Tinosorb). Tinted vs. untinted SPF give essentially the same melanoma protection at the same SPF rating.
- **Melasma / hyperpigmentation is significantly driven by visible light**, particularly high-energy visible (HEV / blue light, 400–450 nm). Iron oxides in tinted SPF block this band; untinted SPF does not.
- For someone with HC + fair skin + sunny climate (high melasma risk profile), **tinted SPF is meaningfully better for melasma prevention** but not for melanoma prevention specifically.

**Recommended tinted SPF brands with strong independent-test records** — see `availability_by_country.md` for current list (La Roche-Posay Anthelios UVMune 400 Tinted, Bioderma Photoderm Cover Touch / Nude Touch).

## Jargon glossary (for inline first-use definitions + output glossary section)

The skill should define these on first use AND maintain a glossary section at the end of the output. Common terms:

| Term | Plain-language definition |
|---|---|
| **Z2 (Zone 2)** | Heart-rate zone ~60–70% of max HR. Aerobic, conversational pace — you can speak in full sentences but with some effort. Mitochondrial-biogenesis zone. |
| **Z3 (Zone 3)** | Heart-rate zone ~70–80% of max HR. Threshold/tempo pace — harder breathing, can speak only short sentences. |
| **Z4 / Z5** | Threshold (Z4, ~80–90%) and max (Z5, >90%) — sprint and near-max effort. |
| **VO2max** | Max rate of oxygen utilization during exercise; predictor of all-cause mortality. ml/kg/min. |
| **MHR** | Max heart rate. Roughly 220 − age, but high individual variation; lab/VO2max tests measure it directly. |
| **MSM** | Musculoskeletal Syndrome of Menopause — Wright's term for the joint-pain / muscle-loss / accelerated osteoarthritis cluster during menopausal transition. |
| **HRT** | Hormone Replacement Therapy (women — estrogen ± progesterone ± testosterone). |
| **TRT** | Testosterone Replacement Therapy (men). |
| **HC** | Hormonal Contraception (general term). |
| **HRV** | Heart Rate Variability — millisecond variation between heartbeats, autonomic-nervous-system health marker. |
| **RHR** | Resting Heart Rate. |
| **MPS** | Muscle Protein Synthesis. |
| **mTOR** | Mechanistic Target of Rapamycin — the muscle-building cellular signaling pathway activated by protein/leucine and resistance training. |
| **BDNF** | Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor — protein involved in neuron growth/maintenance; elevated by vigorous exercise. |
| **NEAT** | Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis — calories burned by daily incidental movement (walking, fidgeting, standing). |
| **MED** | Minimal Erythemal Dose — the UV dose needed to produce a mild sunburn; used in Hollis's vitamin D protocol. |
| **SHBG** | Sex Hormone Binding Globulin — protein that binds sex hormones; affects "free" testosterone available to tissues. |
| **DMT1** | Divalent Metal Transporter 1 — intestinal transporter shared by iron and zinc; the basis of their absorption competition. |
| **Hepcidin** | Hormone that regulates iron absorption; elevated for ≥24 hr after an iron dose, blocking subsequent absorption. |
| **HEV light / blue light** | High-Energy Visible light, ~400–450 nm — drives melasma and visible-light photoaging; blocked by iron oxides in tinted SPF. |
| **Fitzpatrick I–VI** | Skin classification by UV response: I (always burns, never tans) → VI (deeply pigmented, never burns). Drives sun-exposure dosing for vitamin D and SPF need. |
| **EDC** | Endocrine-Disrupting Chemicals — phthalates, bisphenols, parabens, PFAS, etc. |
| **CGM** | Continuous Glucose Monitor — wearable sensor that tracks blood glucose every 1–5 min. |
| **DEXA / DXA** | Dual-energy X-ray Absorptiometry — bone density imaging; T-score and Z-score outputs. |
| **REMS** | Radiofrequency Echographic Multi Spectrometry — radiation-free ultrasound-based bone density imaging. Mostly Italy/EU. |
| **CCC** | Concordance Correlation Coefficient — how closely a wearable's measurement tracks the lab gold standard (1.0 = perfect). |
